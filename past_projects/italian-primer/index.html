Italian: A Primer

A curated collection of Italian language quirks, focusing on false friends, common mistakes, and idiomatic expressions. This is a living document that grows with my Italian learning journey, inspired by discussions in the Italian learning community.

False Friends (Falsi Amici)

False Friend
Attualmente
Looks like: "Actually"
Really means: "Currently, At present"
Correct Italian for "Actually": In realtà
"Attualmente vivo a Bologna" = "I currently live in Bologna"
False Friend
Pretendere
Looks like: "To pretend"
Really means: "To demand, to claim"
Correct Italian for "To pretend": Fingere
"Non puoi pretendere che io sappia tutto" = "You can't demand that I know everything"

Common Mistakes

Common Mistake
Essere vs. Stare
Both mean "to be" but are used differently:
  • Essere: permanent conditions, essential characteristics
  • Stare: temporary states, locations, health conditions
"Sono italiano" (permanent) vs. "Sto male" (temporary)

Colorful Idioms

Idiom
Non avere peli sulla lingua
Literal: "To have no hair on one's tongue"
Means: To speak one's mind, to be frank/direct
"Marco non ha peli sulla lingua, dice sempre quello che pensa" = "Marco speaks his mind, he always says what he thinks"
Idiom
In bocca al lupo
Literal: "Into the wolf's mouth"
Means: Good luck! (Similar to "break a leg")
Response: Crepi! (May it die!)
"Hai un esame domani? In bocca al lupo!" = "You have an exam tomorrow? Good luck!"
This is one of the most common expressions you'll hear in Italy!

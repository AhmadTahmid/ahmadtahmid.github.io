A field guide to economic practice

The Economist’s
Practice Map

Economics is not one craft. It is a family of practices joined by theory, evidence, computation and institutional judgment.

This map asks a practical question: what must an economist be able to do, and how does the answer change by setting? It is an evolving synthesis—not a canonical curriculum or hiring rubric.

01 / Premise

A profession organised around outputs, not software.

The useful unit is not “knows Python” or “studied microeconomics.” It is the capacity to frame a tractable question, choose an identification or modelling strategy, work with imperfect evidence, and communicate a conclusion whose limitations are understood.

Tools therefore appear here in relation to a practice. The same econometric method carries different weight in a central bank forecast, a field experiment, a merger simulation or a marketplace intervention.

04 / Method & limits

How to read a deliberately incomplete map.

01

Relational, not ordinal

The marks describe relevance to a practice, not difficulty, prestige or a universal sequence of study.

02

Roles vary internally

A research economist and a country economist may share an institution while doing materially different work.

03

Tools are perishable

Software changes faster than the underlying capacities: modelling, identification, computation and clear argument.

04

Contestable by design

Every cell is a claim that can be revised. The map should improve through disagreement and domain experience.
